FAQ :

Question 1:  what is the history of Mangaluru Dasara :
Answer : Mangaluru Dasara is a grand celebration that began in the early 20th century and has since grown into one of the most famous festivals of coastal Karnataka. The Kudroli Gokarnanatha Temple, renovated under the guidance of social reformer Narayana Guru in 1912, became a central hub for the festivities. Over the years, the celebrations expanded with processions, tiger dance (Pili Vesha), cultural programs, and beautifully decorated tableaux.

Today, Mangaluru Dasara is known as the “Navaratri of Coastal Karnataka,” drawing thousands of devotees and tourists, while symbolizing unity, devotion, and cultural pride.

Question 2: Are there tableaux/processions during the festival?

Answer : Yes, tableaux and grand processions are a major highlight of Mangaluru Dasara. On Vijayadashami, beautifully decorated tableaux representing mythological and cultural themes parade through the city, accompanied by traditional music, tiger dance (Pili Vesha), and large gatherings of devotees and visitors.

Question 3: Who organizes Mangaluru Dasara?

Answer : Mangaluru Dasara is primarily organized by the Kudroli Gokarnanatha Temple, with active participation from devotees, volunteers, and local cultural groups. The celebrations have grown over the years with support from the community, making it one of the biggest festivals in coastal Karnataka.


Question 4: Why is the festival celebrated?

Answer : Mangaluru Dasara is celebrated as part of Navaratri, a festival dedicated to Goddess Durga and her nine forms. It symbolizes the victory of good over evil and is marked by devotion, cultural programs, tiger dances, and grand processions that bring the community together
